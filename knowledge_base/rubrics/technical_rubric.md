# Technical Interview Scoring Rubric

Use this rubric to evaluate technical interview answers on a 1-5 scale.
Applicable to ANY technical domain — software, data, design, engineering, etc.

## Dimensions

### 1. Clarity Score (1-5)
- **1 — Poor:** Answer is confusing, uses jargon without explanation, or is incoherent.
- **2 — Below Average:** Answer shows some understanding but explanation is muddled or hard to follow.
- **3 — Average:** Answer is understandable but could be more precise. Some ambiguity in explanation.
- **4 — Good:** Answer is well-structured, uses appropriate terminology, and flows logically.
- **5 — Excellent:** Answer is crystal clear — could be used as a teaching example. Complex concepts made accessible.

### 2. Depth Score (1-5)
- **1 — Poor:** Superficial understanding only. Cannot go beyond surface-level definitions.
- **2 — Below Average:** Basic understanding but cannot explain trade-offs, edge cases, or alternatives.
- **3 — Average:** Solid understanding of the core concept. Can discuss basic trade-offs but missing nuance.
- **4 — Good:** Strong understanding. Can discuss trade-offs, alternatives, and edge cases. References real-world applications.
- **5 — Excellent:** Expert-level understanding. Discusses subtleties, historical context, failure modes, and can compare multiple approaches with clear reasoning.

### 3. Technical Accuracy (1-5)
- **1 — Poor:** Multiple factual errors. Fundamental misunderstanding of the concept.
- **2 — Below Average:** Some correct elements but significant errors or misconceptions present.
- **3 — Average:** Mostly accurate with minor errors or imprecise language that doesn't affect core understanding.
- **4 — Good:** Accurate throughout. Any simplifications are appropriate and acknowledged.
- **5 — Excellent:** Completely accurate, precise, and nuanced. Proactively addresses common misconceptions.

### 4. Overall Score (1-5)
Holistic assessment combining clarity, depth, and accuracy, plus:
- Does the candidate demonstrate practical experience (not just textbook knowledge)?
- Can they reason about trade-offs and make justified decisions?
- Do they acknowledge what they don't know honestly?
- Would you trust them to make technical decisions in this area?

## Recommendation Logic
- **overall_score < 3** → recommendation: "probe_deeper" — simplify or offer a hint
- **overall_score == 3** → recommendation: "probe_deeper" or "move_on"
- **overall_score >= 4** → recommendation: "increase_difficulty" or "move_on"
