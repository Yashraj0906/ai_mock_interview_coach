# Situational Interview Question Patterns

These are hypothetical scenario-based questions that test judgment, decision-making, 
and problem-solving. Adaptable to ANY role — the interviewer fills in domain context.

## Pattern: Prioritization & Time Management

### Easy
- "Imagine you have three tasks due by end of day, but you can only realistically complete two. How do you decide which to prioritize?"
- "If you were given a new project on top of your existing workload, how would you handle it?"

### Medium
- "You're working on a high-priority deliverable when your manager asks you to drop everything for an urgent request. How do you handle the conflict?"
- "You realize midway through a project that the approach you've been taking won't work. You've already invested significant time. What do you do?"
- "Your team has 5 feature requests but bandwidth for only 2. Walk me through how you would make the prioritization decision."

### Hard
- "You're leading a project and discover that meeting the deadline means cutting quality, while maintaining quality means missing the deadline. Your stakeholders care about both. How do you handle this?"
- "You inherit a backlog of 20+ items from a predecessor with no documentation. How do you triage, prioritize, and create a plan?"

## Pattern: Stakeholder Management

### Easy
- "A colleague asks for your help on something outside your core responsibilities. How do you respond?"
- "How would you explain a complex topic from your domain to a non-expert stakeholder?"

### Medium
- "A key stakeholder changes requirements midway through a project. How do you handle this?"
- "You receive conflicting directions from two senior people. What's your approach?"
- "You need buy-in from a skeptical stakeholder for a proposal you believe in. How do you approach the conversation?"

### Hard
- "You discover that a project your team shipped has a significant flaw, and a major stakeholder presentation is tomorrow. What do you do?"
- "A strategic initiative you're leading is being deprioritized by leadership, but you believe it's critical. How do you make your case?"

## Pattern: Ethical & Ambiguous Situations

### Easy
- "What would you do if you noticed a colleague taking credit for someone else's work?"
- "How would you handle a situation where you made a mistake that nobody else noticed?"

### Medium
- "You discover that a widely-used process at your company is inefficient but changing it would disrupt many teams. What do you do?"
- "A team member comes to you with concerns about the team's direction but asks you to keep it confidential. How do you handle it?"
- "You're asked to implement something you believe is the wrong approach. How do you handle the situation?"

### Hard
- "You're given a directive that you believe could have negative consequences, but your leadership is firm. How do you navigate this?"
- "You have data that contradicts a strategic decision that's already been communicated to the company. What do you do?"

## Pattern: New Situation Adaptability

### Easy
- "How would you handle your first week in this role? What would your priorities be?"
- "If you joined a new team where you didn't know the domain well, how would you get up to speed?"

### Medium
- "You're put in charge of a project in an area you have minimal experience with. How do you approach it?"
- "Your team's primary tool/platform suddenly becomes unavailable. How do you adapt?"
- "You're asked to work with a team in a different time zone with a 10-hour overlap gap. How do you ensure effective collaboration?"

### Hard
- "You're tasked with building something that has never been done at your company before, with no playbook. Walk me through your approach from day one."
- "The industry landscape shifts dramatically (new regulation, competitor move, technology change). How do you reassess and adapt your team's strategy?"
